01Context

Commissioning a detector meant identifying peaks in its data by hand, dataset by dataset, across thousands of datasets. That took weeks. I built three pieces for that workflow during my internship.

Describe the setting in a few sentences, keeping anything confidential out:

  • What kind of detector, and what "commissioning" involved.
  • What peak identification by hand looked like before your tools: who did it, and how long one dataset took.
  • What you can and can't share, so readers know why there's no source link.

02What I built

2.1

A multithreaded pipeline

Processes 40M+ detector measurements across 64 channels. Compared with the pipeline it replaced, latency fell 85% and throughput rose 10–40×.

2.2

A 12-stage peak-detection framework

Built on methods from CERN's high-energy-physics tooling. 99%+ accuracy, checked with 10+ validation metrics, at under 100 ms per cycle.

2.3

Desktop automation

A tool built on the framework that cut manual peak identification by 90% across 4,000+ datasets.

2.4

Outcome

Detector commissioning went from weeks of manual work to minutes.

  • Pipeline: what was the bottleneck before (file I/O, parsing, a single-threaded loop)? Why did threads work here: was the work I/O-bound, or spent in NumPy calls that release the GIL?
  • Framework: list the 12 stages in one line each (for example baseline estimation, smoothing, candidate search, fit, validation…).
  • Desktop tool: what did it do, and who used it? A screenshot with sensitive values blurred would help a lot.
